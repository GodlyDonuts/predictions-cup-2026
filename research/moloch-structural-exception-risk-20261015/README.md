\*Assumes all other states pay exactly1, zero fees/costs, and the exceptional payout shown. For exception payoutL, expected net is `0.045 − p(1−L)` and break-even `p*=0.045/(1−L)`. With an additional unrecovered per-package costk, replace the numerator by `0.045−k`. These are sensitivity thresholds, **not estimates of any event probability**. Refund values assume newly acquired, fully remaining lots whose actual refundable basis equals their purchase principal. The archived sources do not establish that allocation for every cohort; unknown/inherited basis must not borrow these amounts.

For mutually exclusive state probabilities z (zero payout), a (D-refund/R-loss), b (R-refund/D-loss), c (both refunds), and t (ordinary third-party winner), a conservative expected-net lower bound is:

`EV ≥ 0.045 + t − z − 0.920a − 0.125b − 0.045c − k`.

Other one-refund/one-winning-NO states add upside. Any unmodelled adverse state needs its own payout bound or belongs in the worst-case zero-payout category. Positive EV requires a source-supported bound on this **weighted shortfall**, not a made-up generic “rare event” percentage.

Perfectly coupled cancellation, known cost refunds, no overlap and no adverse override yield a nonnegative basket: ordinary profit or full principal return. They still do not guarantee strictly positive profit in every state, and unrecovered fees/time costs can make refunds a loss. Independent cancellations permit the one-sided losses above. Eliminating fusion alone leaves a0.080 minimum under the stated refund basis, net−0.875; retaining overlap leaves minimum0, net−0.955. Correlation is therefore economically material. Do not assume exception independence or diversify away common sponsor/rule risk. Equal outcome-independent refund marginals can produce equal expected payout under different cancellation correlations while having different tail losses; that algebra is not evidence those assumptions hold here.
