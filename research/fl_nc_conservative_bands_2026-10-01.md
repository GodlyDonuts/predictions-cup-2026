# Florida / North Carolina: conservative decision bands

Prepared 2026-10-01, 19:51 UTC. Public read-only research. Provisional subjective decision analysis for simulated contest points, not a validated probability model or an order. This supplements the earlier holdings forecast dossier, rather than overwriting it.

## Decision

**Do not increase Florida YES at 0.120 or North Carolina NO at 0.065 on a conservative, lower-scenario valuation rule.** Both could have positive expected value under central assumptions, but neither entry price clears plausible downside probability scenarios. Florida's previously attractive 23–27.8% forecast comparison omitted an important range of conflicting recent primary polls.

| Outcome | Subjective working probability band | Illustrative central decision value | Conservative maximum all-in purchase price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Florida Democratic Senate win | 8–30% | roughly 20% | 0.060 |
| North Carolina non-Democratic Senate win | 5–20% | roughly 10% | 0.035 |

The bands are deliberately rounded judgment ranges, **not confidence intervals, backtested calibration, guaranteed floors, or averages of independent models**. The price ceilings implement a disclosed policy: lower working value minus a 2-percentage-point buffer for FL and 1.5-point buffer for NC. They are not estimates of intrinsic value. Costs, fees, and slippage must fit inside the ceilings; both are compatible with the previously verified 0.005 tick. Severe downside scenarios can fall below the bands (approximately 3% FL / 2% NC in the illustration below), so even these prices are not robust to every reasonable scenario.

There is no new recommendation to liquidate existing holdings based on a newly found hobby model. Holding/selling must compare fresh executable bids, the chosen objective, and current portfolio size; entry cost is sunk. In particular, maximizing first-place probability is not the same objective as maximizing expected points or minimizing worst-case loss. No competitor-score distribution was estimated here.

## Newly verified Florida primary polling

### St. Pete Polls: close race, meaningful but imperfect evidence

[September 17 primary PDF](https://stpetepolls.org/files/StPetePolls_2026_State_Gov_September17_G7V29XU6.pdf): Sept 15–17, 913 likely voters, commissioned by Florida Politics. Moody 45.2%, Nixon 44.8%, other 2.9%, undecided 7.1%. Recruitment uses random registered-voter samples; responses are 57% automated phone and 43% peer-to-peer SMS. Weighting matches active voters by party, race, age, gender, and media market, including 42.4% Republican and 30.9% Democratic. Likely-voter exclusion is based on stated voting plans; 5.1% of retained respondents were unsure. Reported sampling MOE is 3.2 points. Inference: evidence for competitiveness exists outside Change Research, but active-registration weights do not guarantee the eventual turnout mix. A poll's share MOE is not the race-win uncertainty.

### Stetson: recent contrary evidence cannot be omitted

[September primary release](https://www.stetson.edu/artsci/cpor/media/cpor-sept.-2026-poll-final.pdf): dated Sept 23, published in the university news feed Sept 24; fielded Sept 14–21. Moody 51%, Nixon 40%, including leaners; other 3%, undecided 5%. **830 is the full adult sample, not a verified likely-voter count.** Qualtrics nonprobability online panel, weighted to Florida adults by age, race, ethnicity, education, gender, and region. Likely voters use modeled past voting and engagement; the LV subset size is absent. Reported MOE is 4 points overall and 4.3 for LV estimates. Moody job approval is 50/32; 32% have not heard of Nixon versus 19% for Moody. Inference: important independent downside evidence, with turnout/panel limitations; labeling it simply “830 LV” overstates what the release establishes.

Overall inference: the evidence strongly favors Cooper. Buying NC NO is a bet on a small, uncertain error/late-change tail, not an inference that the race is close. Polling other-candidate vote shares must not be converted directly into third-party win probabilities.

## Transparent sensitivity arithmetic, not a fitted forecast

A normal margin illustration gives P(win)=Phi(mean margin / error SD). The chosen means/SDs are assumptions, not fitted parameters or polling sampling errors; normal tails themselves can be wrong. It shows why small changes in center/error beliefs matter:

| Event and scenario | Mean winning-side margin | Assumed error SD | Implied probability |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FL D, defensive | -8pp | 6pp | 9.1% |
| FL D, middle | -6pp | 7pp | 19.6% |
| FL D, favorable | -4.4pp | 8pp | 29.1% |
| FL D, severe downside | -11pp | 6pp | 3.3% |
| NC R, defensive | -10pp | 6pp | 4.8% |
| NC R, middle | -8pp | 6pp | 9.1% |
| NC R, favorable | -6pp | 7pp | 19.6% |
| NC R, severe downside | -12pp | 6pp | 2.3% |

Florida's primary polling disagreement and forecast centers motivate approximately -4 to -8 means; the severe row uses the Stetson-sized deficit. NC's large consistent Democratic polling leads motivate approximately -6 to -10 means. These are judgmental bridges from evidence to a decision hurdle. Working bands rounded to8–30% and5–20% retain meaningful uncertainty rather than pretending a precise20%/10% estimate is calibrated.

## Fundamentals and remaining campaign risk

[Florida official 2024 results archived by UCSB](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/election-maps/2024/2024_fl.pdf) show Trump 56.09/Harris 42.99, a 13.10-point all-candidate Republican margin. Florida requires a large swing; national anti-incumbent conditions alone do not make it a toss-up. Moody's incumbency/greater recognition and Nixon's lower recognition matter, but no unsupported numerical candidate-quality adjustment is assigned.

NC has state-level ticket-splitting history and Cooper's statewide experience, so a Republican presidential lean does not erase his observed lead. Current campaign records show [Cooper's September30 affordability/lobbying attack ad](https://roycooper.com/press-releases/) and [Whatley's September campaign events with Vance and attacks on Cooper](https://michaelwhatley.com/news/). These establish active competing campaigns, not truth of campaign allegations or measurable effects. [Nixon's September9 release](https://angienixon.com/ashley-moody-refuses-to-debate-u-s-senate-candidate-angie-nixon-responds/) documents her debate challenge/attack strategy. Ads, turnout shifts, late news and state/national error can still move both races. No allegations are adopted as fact and no polling-point effect is assigned.
