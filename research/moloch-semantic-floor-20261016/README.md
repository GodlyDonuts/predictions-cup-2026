## What can actually be proved

**Immediate same-market netting:** an opposite BUY consumes held opposite shares and credits one SUSQie per paired share. It is a cash operation, independent of which election party later wins. For newly buying both sides of the same canonical book, YES ask is `a` and NO ask is `1−b`, where `b` is the YES bid. Cost is `1+(a−b)`, at least one on an uncrossed book. Full-depth execution cannot improve this inequality: further asks cost at least `a`, and complemented deeper bids cost at least `1−b`. Full gross funding, actual fees and atomic execution remain necessary. A currently crossed executable book would be a separate empirical opportunity requiring fresh original depth; none was observed or asserted here.

Exact integer arithmetic checked all19,900 noncrossed0.005-tick BBO pairs with prices strictly between zero and one: zero positive redemption returns, maximum zero before fees. This is an algebra check, not an actual book scan or a financial integration test. Passive bids whose combined prices fall below one do not establish simultaneous fills; queue, missing-leg and adverse-selection risk remain. An opposite BUY closing an existing position can realize a gain over its attributed FIFO cost, but that gain must be evaluated from actual inventory and executable depth; it is not an independent two-leg arbitrage.

**Hypothetical terminal complement:** ordinary YES/NO outcomes pay a combined one. A documented cancellation/N/A returns remaining refundable principal. If an all-principal-refund state is admissible for a newly acquired package, its net payoff is zero before fees and negative after nonrefunded fees. A strictly positive minimum over all admissible states is then impossible. This terminal calculation must not be applied to a pair already extinguished by immediate netting.

**Different party markets:** a common race/stage and different party names do not prove mutual exclusion under the actual resolver. Even granting ordinary exclusion, asymmetric cancellation can defeat the proposed one-unit floor. For the historical Michigan dual-NO costs0.105 and0.885, refunding the first while the other NO loses returns0.105 against0.990 cost: **−0.885**. Refunding both returns principal: **zero**, not+0.010. These are source-compatible stress cases not proved impossible by the retained evidence; their probabilities are not estimated. Original Michigan node receipts remain unavailable in the local historical review, so it cannot become a current certificate.
