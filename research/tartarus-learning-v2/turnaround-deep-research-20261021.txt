My decision is: keep Rust as the sole live account/execution/ledger authority, add only a limited Python shadow/offline strategy layer, and do not undertake a broad rewrite. The highest-value work is not language migration. It is, in order: remove the known decision-path latency pathology; finish safe V3 recurring-cohort activation; then test an event-driven, edge-gated, immediately executable turnover strategy across all markets. The current Rust stack contains hard-won properties—exact FIFO accounting, unknown-write reconciliation, durable idempotency, stream recovery, one-account rate ownership and replay—that a rewrite would have to recreate before it could improve a single economic decision. Recent R71/R72 failures also show how expensive apparently small lifecycle mistakes can be.

The new target of at least one distinct exchange-confirmed filled order per minute is mechanically possible under the API budget, but it is not presently demonstrated to be economically feasible. Current official documentation says the budget is account-wide at 100 reads and 30 writes/minute, shared across API keys; production's self-imposed 24-write/minute pacing is therefore conservative rather than an exchange rule.
1
 The problem is fill probability. The repository's best actual passive-order calibration found that only 41 of 891 ordinary BUY orders obtained any fill, about 4.60%. The alternative is not “cross more spreads until the counter says 60.” SIG is peer-to-peer: execution requires compatible liquidity, and self-trade prevention prevents your own resting orders from satisfying your incoming order. Selling an unowned side is simply the complementary economic position, not free short leverage; the competition is cash-only.

Two restrictions deserve explicit classification. In fundamental_policy.rs, SameSideTopUps.maximum_entries is constrained by repository code to 2–16, and eligibility stops once the cumulative entry count reaches that limit; c.top_ups.len() >= 128 is another hard eligibility stop. These are implementation/risk-policy constraints, not SIG exchange limits. At most, 12 completely unused 16-entry cohorts represent 192 acquisitions before this lifetime cap; prior acquisitions reduce that number. This alone makes 16 a poor long-run representation of a strategy intended to cycle repeatedly.

I would not simply raise 16 and 128 today. The 50MB financial structures, 2.56GB replay and restart cost show that “just retain more historical objects” can worsen the problem. The right successor is a rolling risk bound, not a lifetime trade bound:

text
Copy
admit new cohort entry iff
    unresolved_physical_orders == 0
    AND active_entry_count < max_active_entries
    AND held_quantity + new_quantity <= max_quantity
    AND held_cost + reserved_cost <= max_principal
    AND daily_realized_loss > -loss_limit
    AND current source/review facts are fresh


Every historical order should remain in the authoritative append-only ledger, but fully reconciled closed-cycle proof should be represented to the hot decision path by a compact immutable checkpoint/index rather than by an ever-growing vector that is rescanned each time. The 128-slot hot-history limit is therefore a reasonable memory-defense symptom, but not an economically sensible lifetime turnover ceiling.

What one fill per minute really requires

Under the historical ordinary-passive fill rate:

[ p_{\text{fill}}=\frac{41}{891}\approx4.60% ]

and therefore:

[ E[\text{placements per filled order}]\approx21.73. ]

With all 24 production write slots used as placements:

[ 24\times0.0460\approx1.10 \text{ filled orders/min}. ]

With a safer 18 entry/reprice writes and six writes retained for exits/cancels/recovery:

[ 18\times0.0460\approx0.83 \text{ filled orders/min}. ]

Those calculations are deliberately optimistic: they treat the old conditional mix as stationary and independent, ignore cancellations, and say nothing about profit. The underlying calibration explicitly warns that its historical closure-conditioned fractions are not a calibrated future passive fill probability.

Thus the human's ≥1/min requirement is feasible only if at least one of these becomes true:

there are routinely enough marketable, positive-edge opportunities for conditional fill probability to be much higher than 4.6%;
a much better passive filter materially raises fill probability and improves post-fill markout; or
safe batch/multi-leg execution captures multiple independently worthwhile trades without overwhelming cancellation and inventory controls.

The decisive pre-production test is therefore:

[ \lambda_{\rm edge}\times p_{\rm fill|edge} \ge 1.25/\text{minute} ]

over rolling 30-minute shadow windows, where (\lambda_{\rm edge}) counts independent, semantically valid, executable-depth, net-positive opportunities, not quote updates. I recommend 1.25 rather than 1.0 as headroom for timing variance. Failure of that test means “one profitable fill/min” is not currently supportable by observed liquidity; production must not manufacture activity to meet the KPI.

Strategy feasibility on this exact venue

Inventory-aware passive market making: technically feasible, economically unproven, cadence-poor in its current form. SIG permits resting both sides while preventing self-trading.
3
 But historical Moloch ordinary passive orders had very low fill incidence and the sparse executable post-fill marks were negative. Do not reactivate a broad maker simply because 60 fills/hour is desired. A maker quote belongs only where expected spread capture exceeds fill-conditioned adverse movement, likely unwind cost, inventory carry and the shadow value of a write.

Inventory liquidation: immediately feasible and probably the best source of near-term genuine SELL legs, but not automatically profitable. The new human-verified 993 YES sale is concrete evidence that at least one sizable inventory exit could cross at positive FIFO realized gross. The preceding three exits lost a combined 105.925, so liquidation quality varies sharply. The strategy should rank owned inventory by marginal expected benefit of exiting now, not by age alone and not by a blanket “only sell above FIFO” rule.

Fast external-reference dislocation trading: the best near-term alpha candidate, but only after semantic certification. Kalshi's API exposes primary/secondary rules and market expiration/settlement metadata, enabling much stronger matching than names alone.
9
 Polymarket's own documentation describes its 0–1 outcome shares and $1 winning outcome, but that still does not prove that any given Polymarket question settles identically to a SIG contract.
10
 The external venue should therefore be treated as information only when all of outcome definition, resolver/source, date boundary, cancellation/N/A behavior, geography/candidate identity and relevant runoff rules have been explicitly mapped.

Complement/complete-set conversion: ordinary same-market “YES + NO < 1” is not a separate hidden conversion strategy here. SIG already defines YES and NO as complements and automatically nets opposite holdings.
3
 The inspected participant API does not expose an explicit mint/merge/redeem operation. Any apparent complement edge must exist in actual peer depth after this matching algebra, not in a synthetic API feature.

Cross-market relationships: potentially useful, but older research overstated its implementation readiness. The October 15 paper's relationship/collateral thesis was directionally interesting, because the repository really does contain relationship and collateral-read machinery. But a relationship metadata endpoint is not itself a settlement-floor certificate, and a collateral GET is not a participant mint/borrow operation. The near-term valid use is to screen for logically related contracts, verify every payoff state and then trade only if ordinary/multi-leg execution can lock a positive executable payoff. Treat the old “package arbitrage” proposal as dated hypothesis, not a currently implementable free-money path.

Traditional HFT claims are impossible here. Market updates are approximately second-batched, account writes are 30/minute, production intentionally uses 24/minute, and liquidity is peer-to-peer.

 A 60–120 second worker is far too slow; a 200-microsecond worker is unnecessary. The useful target is sub-second local decision processing after a received versioned batch, followed by only the minimum authoritative exchange reads/writes.

Minimal profitable-turnover candidate

I would test one strategy before adding more architecture: an edge-gated executable turnover router. “Profitable” here is a falsification criterion, not a promise.

Its purpose is to create genuine BUY→SELL→REBUY cycles only when a conservative fair-value interval says the currently executable SIG price is wrong enough to pay for crossing and adverse selection.

Market selection

Maintain all 237 SIG markets from realtime. Do not run a 60–120 second monolithic “scope review” before knowing whether the market deserves work. The repository already has scheduling primitives that prioritize urgent owned scopes and interleave owned/fresh work, without granting extra execution authority.

For every new SIG book version, update a cheap in-memory candidate:

[ C_m = (\text{SIG BBO/depth}, \text{inventory}, \text{matched external refs}, \text{source ages}, \text{semantic mapping id}). ]

A market is trade-eligible only when:

[ \text{mapping_verified}=1, \quad \text{book_fresh}=1, \quad \text{external_fresh}=1, \quad \text{unknown_order_state}=0. ]

The mapping itself should be an immutable versioned record. Similar title strings should never set mapping_verified.

Fair-value interval

Do not pretend the external midpoint is truth. Construct an uncertainty interval:

[ \hat f = \operatorname{weightedMedian} (p_{\rm Kalshi}, p_{\rm Polymarket}, p_{\rm simple\ forecast}) ]

using only sources actually available at that causal timestamp, then define:

[ u = u_{\rm venue\ disagreement} + u_{\rm staleness} + u_{\rm settlement} + u_{\rm model}, ]

[ f_L=\max(0,\hat f-u),\qquad f_U=\min(1,\hat f+u). ]

The first production experiment should use simple static calibration, not PPO. A source whose mapping or contemporaneous availability is uncertain gets zero weight.

where (\lambda_{\rm inv}) is positive when reducing unwanted inventory has independent risk value.

For NO, transform the probability consistently rather than letting the action API accidentally convert an intended exit into a new complementary BUY.

The initial shadow threshold should be deliberately demanding: at least 0.010 SUSQie/share—two SIG ticks—after all buffers, plus a minimum expected order-level gain such as 0.50 SUSQie. Those are test parameters, not empirically proven optimums. They prevent one-share “fill farming” from satisfying the cadence metric without useful economics.

Size and price

For each executable level:

[ q = \min\left( hD,; Q_{\rm risk},; \frac{N_{\rm max}}{p},; Q_{\rm inventory} \right) ]

where (D) is displayed external counterparty depth at the accepted SIG price, and (h) begins at a conservative haircut such as 0.5 until actual depth survival is measured.

Write-budget controller

[ S_i = \frac{ q_i e_i p_{{fill},i} }{ 1+\eta_w w_i+\eta_c K_i T_i }, ]

\frac{ |{\text{distinct official order IDs with quantity filled}>0}| }{ \text{active minutes} }. ]

Architecture and implementation seam

A broad Python rewrite is the wrong turnaround.

The current repository has a very large Rust live worker plus separate modules for authority, dispatch, execution edge/routing, maker decisions, immutable order snapshots, scheduling, relationship/package work and stream recovery. Python is already extensively used in moloch/learning_actions, so there is no language-policy reason the strategy research layer itself must be Rust.

What must stay in Rust

Keep these live-authoritative responsibilities where they are:

authority.rs / production authority; dispatch.rs / durable intents and unknown acceptance; account_realtime.rs; order_snapshot.rs; fundamental_policy.rs; native FIFO/session/replay; REST/realtime ownership and the shared account limiter.

Those are precisely the components whose failure can duplicate a write, corrupt a ledger assumption or race another account operation. Moving them does not create alpha.

Copy
fn existing_target_busy(
    bank: &Session,
    log: &DispatchLog,
    exchange: &str,
) -> Result<bool> {
    log.require_resolved_attempts()?;

    let snapshot = moloch_live_runtime::order_snapshot::snapshot(bank);

    for id in log.acknowledgments.keys().filter(|id| !log.closed.contains(id)) {
        let q = snapshot
            .quote(*id)
            .ok_or("LIVE_ACK_QUOTE_MISSING")?;

        if q["exchangeId"] == exchange {
            return Ok(true);
        }
    }

    Ok(snapshot.records().iter().any(|q| {
        q["exchangeId"] == exchange
            && q["retired"] == false
            && (
                q.get("reviewedInventoryExit").is_some()
                || q.get("reviewedFundamentalEntry").is_some()
                || q["id"].as_u64().is_some_and(|id| {
                    bank.engine().is_package_order(id)
                        || !log.never_attempted(id)
                })
            )
    }))

ML methodology repair

The current ML result is not merely “the network needs more training.” The latest completed population member produced −10.56343% nominal marked ROI, +0.0030525% stressed, 15/2 fills, all BUY_NO and zero realized FIFO gain. There is, however, evidence that the historical SIG dataset contains price movement worth attempting to discover: a fit-only perfect-hindsight census found 327 exchange-sides across 190 exchanges with a positive one-share ask-to-later-bid move over 3 seconds to 1 hour, with a maximum screened move of 0.06/share. The immediate ML question is instead whether causal policy learning can find enough of those opportunities after execution constraints.

Separate four failure classes

Action starvation. Count, at every decision, how many legal BUY, SELL, WAIT and target choices exist; then report how frequently the policy assigns each family probability and chooses it. SELL-against-zero-inventory transformations must be categorized by resulting economic exposure, not by nominal API verb. The current repository has already observed SELL selections turning into complementary BUY exposure.

Policy optimization bugs. Compare frozen founder, trained descendant and the same network with randomized action heads on identical worlds/seeds. Founder retention is sensible engineering, but “least losing survivor” is not evidence of learned edge.

Simulator mistakes. Replay actual production orders through the simulator and require agreement on: legal action, API admission time, available book, partial quantities, FIFO inventory, cash, order expiry and resulting markout. The 26 reviewed historical orders that were already marketable must not be mixed into passive queue calibration.

No usable signal. Only after mechanics and simple baselines pass should failure to outperform cash/static policies be interpreted as evidence that the available predictors do not justify trading.

What not to do

Do not chase 60 fills/hour by crossing negative expected spread, reducing every trade to one-share cadence farming, batch-spraying dozens of passive orders, self-trading, spoofing, wash-like cycling or manufacturing marks. SIG explicitly prohibits unfair/manipulative activity and its matching logic prevents self-trades in any case.
1

Do not add another official-account client, API key strategy or process intended to multiply rate capacity. The limits are account-wide.
6

Do not rewrite or truncate the original financial state. The current system's 2.56GB replay cost is painful, but preserving authoritative historical state is more important than shortening startup.

Do not deploy PPO because stressed marked ROI happened to be barely positive while nominal ROI was deeply negative, all fills were BUY_NO and realized FIFO gain was zero.

Do not describe relationship metadata or /portfolio/collateral as a proven participant conversion/arbitrage API. The inspected surface establishes reads; the participant write primitive needed for a generic mint/merge strategy was not verified.

Do not treat BBO bulk prices as executable depth. The repository validator itself explicitly avoids that inference.

Do not count an ACK as a fill, a partial receipt as an independent order, invested principal as P&L, or official marked inventory as immediately liquidatable cash.
