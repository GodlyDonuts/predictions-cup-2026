When reference-price edge can support turnover

It can support turnover when the external signal predicts a subsequent change in SIG executable bids, at the relevant size and within the required holding horizon. Evidence would include actual size-qualified bid improvement, recurring contra-side replenishment, and exits that survive measured submission delays.

A large reference discount can also justify holding to settlement, but that is a different economic use of capital. SIG pays winning positions at settlement; markets can remain pending until valid results arrive. That payout mechanism is not an hourly cash-recycling facility.

When it cannot

A reference discount alone does not establish near-term turnover when SIG prices remain persistently different, bid depth is thin, the reference mapping is uncertain, or the entry fills precisely when the apparent edge is deteriorating.

Repeatedly buying the same persistent discount can simply exhaust cash. Repeatedly realizing old low-cost winners while retaining deteriorating positions can produce positive realized FIFO statistics without growing the account.
