## Independent polling and pitfalls

### Alaska: best verified primary poll document

AARP commissioned bipartisan Fabrizio Ward/Impact Research; fieldwork **Sep 8–11**, 800 likely voters, released Sep 17. Methods: 40% live cellphone, 15% live landline, 45% SMS-to-web, randomly drawn from Alaska voter list, reported MOE ±3.5%. Primary publisher search-index content verified these methods: 
https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2026-09-17-new-aarp-alaska-poll-older-voters-will-be-influential-in-u-s-senate-gubernatorial-and-congressional-races/

Direct AARP page opens returned 403. The actual AARP report, including DOI and publisher branding, was retrieved through DDHQ's linked poll-document mirror: 
https://data.ddhq.io/polls/2026/09/17/Fabrizio-Ward/Impact-Research/AARP-Alaska-2026-09-08-2026-09-11

- Senate report p 3: initial Peltola 46/Sullivan 41/other Sullivan 4/Heikes 3/undecided 7; after RCV Peltola 53/Sullivan 47. **11% do not rank either finalist**, and that group voted Trump by 40 points. These are final-round shares, not win probabilities.
- Governor report p 5: initial JKT 40/Wilson 20/Bronson 14/Taylor 7/undecided 18; final JKT 55/Wilson 45. **25% do not rank either finalist**; that group voted Trump by 41 points. Consolidation could materially change the outcome.
- This one poll supports both Alaska races and therefore cannot be treated as two independent observations. Ballot exhaustion and turnout assumptions are central.

### Alaska counterpoll

https://battlegrounds.coefficient.org/ak/

Primary page retrieved Oct 1 around 19:49 UTC. Fielded **Sep 14–17**, 799 likely voters, mobile text/landline, reported MOE ±3.45. Initial Sullivan 48/Peltola 46/other Sullivan 1/Heikes 1/undecided 4; separate head-to-head Sullivan 49/Peltola 46/undecided 5. It is not the same procedure as AARP's simulated RCV. Page says independently funded, not candidate/party/independent-expenditure sponsored; co/efficient is a Republican-associated firm.

**Material composition flag:** its published profile has 44% aged 65+, 23% aged 55–64 and 4% aged 18–34. It says age/party/etc weighting was applied. Treat as a turnout-composition-sensitive counterpoll, not decisive pro-Sullivan evidence. AARP finds Peltola much stronger among younger voters.

### Kansas

https://emersoncollegepolling.com/kansas-2026/

Released **Sep 18**, fielded **Sep 15–16**, 750 likely voters, English text-to-web/voter-file matched panel, reported credibility interval ±3.6. Hamilton 45/Marshall 43/Graham 4/undecided 8. Hamilton leads independents 42–27. Graham supporters favor Republican Masterson 65–31 in the simultaneous governor question, creating potential consolidation risk for Hamilton. The small lead, undecideds, red-state baseline and one poll do not justify a majority win probability. DDHQ also lists Wedgewood 50–48 (Sep 22–24) and co/efficient Marshall 49–44 (Sep 8–10), but their original documents were not independently checked here; do not promote those listings to primary evidence.
