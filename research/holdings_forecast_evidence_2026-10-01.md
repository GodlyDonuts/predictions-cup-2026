## Main conclusion

Florida merits the highest research priority: two published forecasts put Nixon materially above the historical 0.12 entry cost. NC NO also remains plausible as a cheap tail position, but the sources disagree substantially about its probability. RI/MA have supportive safe-seat evidence and limited absolute upside. None of this establishes a fresh executable opportunity or warrants increasing positions before execution/model checks.

**DDHQ explicitly assigns 25% of each of the four holdings' forecasts to prediction markets. It is not independent market-free confirmation.** Its [2026 methodology](https://votes-docs.decisiondeskhq.com/forecast-methodology/2026-congressional-forecast-methodology?utm_campaign=faq-methodologies&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=www.decisiondeskhq.com) identifies Kalshi and Polymarket as market inputs and describes probability-level blending. Do not combine DDHQ, those markets and VoteHub Complete as independent evidence. Exact unrounded input prices and timing would be required to remove the market component algebraically; those were not retrieved.

## Primary polling checks

### Florida

[Change Research, September 28 release](https://changeresearch.com/research/2026/2026-09-28-florida-aapor-methodology-statement): fieldwork September 25–27, Nixon 47/Moody 48. Freedom Project USA commissioned it. English-only online recruitment and its conflicting likely-voter counts (1,063 versus 1,107) are material data-quality flags. It supports competitiveness as a hypothesis; it should not independently establish a win probability or a precise weight. Publication recency must not conceal sponsor/method issues.

[UNF PORL primary July release](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6994d571bce3436c60d86870/t/6a637f8ec8249053ae278f02/1784905614403/UNF%2BPORL%2BSummer%2BStatewide%2B2026%2BFLLV%2B-%2BPress%2BRelease.pdf): fieldwork July 8–17, 848 likely midterm voters, Moody 50/Nixon 42. Self-funded mixed phone/text-to-web survey, in English and Spanish. This is cleaner independent polling evidence but much older, and was fielded before the primary. Its Nixon matchup is relevant; other hypothetical matchups are not interchangeable.

### North Carolina

[Catawba/YouGov primary September 30 release](https://www.catawba.edu/news/all-news/2026/yougov-39/): fieldwork September 4–15. Cooper 47/Whatley 35 among 885 likely voters; 48/37 including leaners. Thus the headline's September 30 date does not mean yesterday's voter preferences. The strong Democratic lead is directionally consistent across the two forecasts, while uncertainty about tail error explains why their win probabilities can differ sharply.

### Massachusetts and Rhode Island

DDHQ displays UNH poll listings consistent with sizable Democratic leads. The linked poll documents did not resolve through the web tool, so those listings were not independently reverified at the original publisher. Do not promote them to primary-source-confirmed inputs. The available model evidence is more useful as a broad safe-seat classification than as proof that a 0.1–1% loss tail is precisely known.

## Interpretation for the existing positions

- **Florida YES:** both model snapshots support reviewing the original thesis rather than selling solely because of a portfolio mark. A supported downside sensitivity must address polling sponsor effects, sparse independent recent polling, correlated national error, and DDHQ's market input. This is the strongest existing candidate for additional research, not permission to chase any ask.
- **NC NO:** a likely Democratic victory can coexist with an underpriced Republican/other-winner tail. However, a few probability points are a large relative change in a cheap contract. Evaluate the exact party-NO predicate, not only Whatley's candidate probability. The substantial difference between the two forecasts argues for small confidence in a precise point estimate.
- **MA/RI YES:** ordinary-election evidence supports the safe-seat premise, while their combined maximum entry-to-settlement gain remains only 134.35 SUSQies. Poor exit depth can make a sale unattractive despite low first-place contribution. Current costs, depth and opportunity cost belong in Drogon's decision layer.
- **Chamber control:** 54% versus 71.7% Democratic Senate control is a warning against treating one model's national scenario as certain. Review covariance, seat map, independents and tie/control definitions before using control exposure alongside state races.
