# Diversified historical dip paper strategy

The [official API v1](https://sig.thesuper.market/api/v1/openapi.json) provides `GET /exchanges/{id}/price-history`. A current public specification retrieved October 2 confirms tournament-scoped YES-normalized OHLCV, sparse missing-trade buckets, one-minute through weekly resolutions, and a maximum 1,000 candles per request. The local collector requests 500 closed one-minute candles with an explicit tournament and floored exclusive end. It records the actual served range and projection coverage; it does not claim a complete lifetime history or fill missing periods.

The independent wallet starts after its frozen journal cutoff. Historical observations from that prefix seed references only; they never become retrospective trades. Ongoing candle responses and full book observations are appended to the existing durable hash-chained journal. Each request shares the single collector's serialized admission, persisted Retry-After and STOP behavior. No separate market client is started.

The first fixed policy uses the median of closed historical candle VWAPs over six hours, or prior unique verified book midpoints when enough closed candles are unavailable. These reference sources remain distinguishable. It needs eight samples across at least 30 minutes and rejects stale references. A candidate must be at least two cents and 3% below its historical reference, pass a robust deviation threshold, retain a spread no wider than two cents, and avoid a drop greater than ten cents. The actual full-depth request rechecks that discount after adverse ticks. This is a mean-reversion hypothesis, not a claim that the historical median is fair value or that prices must recover after news.

Each paper entry is up to 25 shares and 50 SUSQies. There are at most 12 concurrent market bets, two per state and six per office grouping. Only one market with incomplete grouping metadata can be open. Total cost exposure is capped at 5,000 of the 100,000 bankroll, with 95,000 reserved. No averaging into an existing position. Pending entries count against concentration caps and are revoked after missing books, account outages or a vanished discount. A fully exited market has a 15-minute cooldown. These caps limit concentration; political outcomes remain correlated.

Profit-taking triggers at one cent above actual average entry cost, stop-loss at three cents below it, and maximum holding time is one hour. Both buys and sells require a subsequent observed book, decision latency, displayed-depth fractions, adverse ticks and exact FIFO accounting. Additional explicit fees are zero. A price touching the target never creates a fill. A current intent, pending exit, partial exit or stale mark remains visible and cannot count as a completed win.

The new wallet distinguishes a fresh authoritative REST read from the book's last engine-event clock. The official book version records the last applied event, so a quiet book can have an old event clock. Request latency, exact tournament, exchange identity, version validity/ordering, exclusion of real account holdings/orders and valid depth are still enforced. Event age remains recorded. Frozen previous wallets retain their prior event-age freshness rules. Unchanged-version depth can serve a delayed first entry once; consumed depth cannot be repeatedly reused.

The collector shares four scheduled book reads between managed positions, candidates, original comparison markets and discovery. It also refreshes historical references and bulk prices through the same admission queue. The separately recorded sole-client network budget permits three-second completion spacing; frozen strategy policies stay unchanged. Candidate plans are bank-bound and require a recent running worker heartbeat. Held positions and pending intents take priority over new leads. Historical reads can proceed before account reconciliation, but every fillable book requires that cycle's account exclusions.

Core and supplemental book snapshots are merged in receipt order before advancing a wallet. Array membership does not imply collection order: a managed or candidate market can be fetched before a core comparison market. The October 2 repair preserves the original source journal, cohort cutoffs, bank hashes and saved wallet accounting, and resumes from the last committed source record. Replayed observations after a worker outage remain delayed local simulations, not actual competition fills.

Start the independent worker using `Start-Dip.ps1`; view current realized P&L, hypothetical fills, completed round trips, win rate, open positions, pending exits and collection health at `http://127.0.0.1:8787` or `/api/dip`. History, state, model banks and journal stay in the restricted LOCALAPPDATA runtime. Tests establish implementation correctness; only subsequent actual observations can establish performance. These wallets submit no orders. The separately authorized bounded official pilot is described in [execution/README.md](../execution/README.md).

The separate `dip-scale` cohort preserves that first experiment and uses an independent future cutoff and wallet. It allows up to 100 concurrent markets, four per state, 50 per office grouping, and one unknown identity. Entries are at most 1,000 shares and 1,000 SUSQies (1% of initial bankroll); aggregate cost is capped at 50,000 with 50,000 reserved. Real depth and signals can result in far fewer trades. Both workers share the same two candidate-depth requests; holdings and pending intents are prioritized. Start it with `Start-Dip.ps1 -CohortName dip-scale`. No extra API client or forced trade is introduced.

The read-only Next.js frontend is at `http://127.0.0.1:3000`; see `frontend/README.md` and `Start-Dashboard.ps1`. Both wallet tabs show positions, recent fills, pending entries/exits, current P&L and worker/collector health. FIFO performance additionally records completed episodes, partial realized P&L, open cost basis, inventory ages and cost-weighted returns. A partial profitable exit cannot hide a remaining losing position in the win-rate denominator.
