# Stable spread paper strategy — October 5

Baal now joins a stable best bid instead of crossing the spread to buy a dip. After a hypothetical buy it offers the share one tick higher; a later depth-checked bid may also allow an exit. This is an implemented counterfactual strategy, not a profitable official bot.

The original frozen continuation/reversal study was extended on subsequent observed quotes. Every one of 36 fixed side/horizon/latency cases remained negative: mean quoted net markouts ranged from -0.032917 to -0.008000 per share with 5–30 completed observations per case. The 2.758-day quote span contains an approximately eleven-hour gap. Cases share signals; these are neither independent trades nor executed returns. Fees were zero. See [original measured result](../data/native-mechanisms-future-1791205489070.json).

The new policy uses four minutes of observations within a five-minute stable-price window, a three-second activation delay, a two-minute quote lifetime, one-share entries, a one-tick (0.005) profit target, a 0.030 loss trigger and a two-hour holding limit. These are design assumptions, not fitted profitable parameters. Separate YES and NO wallets start with 100 each and remain correlated. A price touch or disappearing book quantity cannot create a fill: the model requires a subsequent anonymous trade strictly through the quote. Anonymous prints still do not establish our queue position. Exact fully paginated coverage through the later book checkpoint resolves expired quotes; incomplete coverage preserves cash/inventory reservations. Covered exits, exact cash/FIFO accounting and restart frontiers are durable.

The earlier prefixes are labeled development. Wider-spread V1 generated no quotes. One-tick V2 produced 58 buy quotes and one unclosed hypothetical purchase, with no realized gain; it exposed missing original tape windows rather than permission to assume a fill.
