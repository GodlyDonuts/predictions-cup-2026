# Adopted portfolio read capacity review — October 15

[Task #134](https://github.com/GodlyDonuts/predictions-cup-2026/issues/134) reviews the parent's worker correction: construct `Universe` using the replayed `bank.engine().policy()["maximumMarkets"]` instead of the original control policy. The change aligns REST selection capacity with actual managed holdings. It does not change financial limits or enlarge the allocation to fresh markets.

Verified adoption in `engine_live.rs` increases effective `maximumMarkets` by the count of newly attributed inherited markets and separately retains the original `fresh_market_limit`. With28 adopted markets and an original limit3, these are31 managed markets and3 fresh markets. `Engine::required_exchanges()` includes both held inventory and unresolved orders. Native quote admission applies both the effective overall limit and the independent count of fresh markets. The worker passes only the managed limit to the read selector; cash, per-market cost, total committed funds, size, activation, shared rate budget and native quote admission are unaffected by that argument.

The new `managed_read_capacity.rs` integration tests submit an actual28-position adoption event to the real native engine. They check effective capacity31 versus original3, preserved fresh limit3, unchanged monetary/size/write/age limits, exact opening principal/cash and no fabricated orders or fills. Four distinct fresh book events then prove only three new funded market obligations are admitted while all28 inherited markets remain required. This proves the native distinction; it does not by itself test the private worker's wiring or establish a live latency result.

The original selector capacity3 rotates a28-market owned portfolio over many outer cycles, reserving a discovery slot when candidates are present. The correction permits all28 inherited markets plus up to three candidates in one selection. When31 markets are already required and a new candidate exists, the existing fairness rule selects30 owned markets plus one discovery candidate, rotating the omitted owned market next time. Capacity31 is not a guarantee that every required market is selected every cycle. The change removes the accidental use of a fresh-entry count as the read-coverage cap.

There is a scheduling tradeoff. `source()` performs at least a trades GET and a book GET per selected scope. A31-scope packet therefore costs at least62 REST reads, or37.2 seconds of the600ms completion gap even with zero HTTP latency. Pagination, retries, the rolling100-read budget, owned-order reconciliation, six-read dispatch preflights and writes add time. The outer loop reconciles owned orders once **before** the entire selected packet. It drains realtime and attempts dispatch after each scope, but those calls do not refresh all owned fill tapes again. A larger packet can delay the next full owned reconciliation and status update even while book coverage improves.

The correction is justified, but it is not a complete latency improvement. The next scheduler step should keep the effective managed capacity for eligibility while executing bounded prioritized chunks: prioritize owned pending orders and urgent covered exits, interleave full owned reconciliation at measured deadlines, and retain fair rotation/discovery without adding clients or relaxing receipt/risk checks. Measure complete-cycle duration, owned-fill age and per-market book revisit time before claiming a speed gain. This review makes no shared source, runtime, API or order change.
